# Slalom HTML Deck Visual World

This file describes the practical visual world for Slalom HTML slide decks. Use it to keep generated decks from collapsing into generic web pages, generic AI infographics, or repetitive starter layouts.

## Core Atmosphere

Slalom HTML decks should feel:

- executive-readable
- structured and precise
- visually modern without becoming trendy
- confident with whitespace
- grounded in enterprise delivery reality
- clearly Slalom-branded without overdecorating every slide

The default canvas is white or very light. Slalom Blue provides the anchor. Dark blue fields are useful for resets and closes. Accent colors should encode meaning, not fill space.

## White Canvas

Use white canvas when the slide needs:

- executive clarity
- diagrams, matrices, or roadmaps
- solution architecture or operating model content
- proof strips or evidence boards
- client-facing professionalism

Good white-canvas slides often use:

- bold black claim headline
- selective Slalom Blue emphasis
- precise top-left eyebrow
- thin blue rails, dividers, or spine lines
- light gray structural fields
- one secondary accent family at a time

Avoid empty white slides where the only design is text plus three boxes.

## Blue Field Resets

Use Slalom Blue or Slalom Dark Blue fields when the deck needs:

- a chapter break
- a decisive ask
- a strong close
- a short editorial statement
- a high-confidence recommendation

Blue field slides should be sparse. Use white type, chartreuse or cyan only as controlled accents, and avoid dense tables on full blue unless the slide is intentionally a dashboard-style artifact.

## Editorial Headlines

Headlines should be claims, not labels.

Good:

- "Governance makes the system safe to scale, not slower to use."
- "Licenses create potential; operating model creates throughput."
- "Start where delivery risk is high and evidence is fragmented."

Weak:

- "Overview"
- "Approach"
- "Key Considerations"
- "AI Transformation Framework"

Use sentence case. Let the headline carry the point so the visual can encode the relationship.

## Proof Strips

Proof strips work when a slide needs to connect a claim to concise supporting evidence.

Use:

- 3-5 proof points maximum
- one highlighted point when there is a recommendation
- short labels and one-line implications
- a bottom note only when it clarifies the commercial or delivery meaning

Avoid proof strips that become equal feature cards. If every proof point has the same weight, add a hierarchy signal.

## Operating Model Diagrams

Operating model diagrams should show relationships, not noun collections.

Useful forms:

- central decision spine with loops around it
- layered platform stack
- role/loop matrix
- workstream swimlane
- lifecycle rail with handoffs
- governance rhythm with feedback loops

Every model should answer: what changes, who owns it, and how work moves.

## Governance Tables

Governance tables are acceptable when the table itself is the clearest form.

Make them scannable with:

- strong header row
- loop or workstream labels in blue
- short verbs
- one row per decision area
- no paragraph-heavy cells

For executive decks, keep tables to 3-5 rows and 3-4 columns unless the slide is explicitly an appendix or working artifact.

## Roadmaps And Paths

Roadmaps should show movement and priority, not just sequence.

Use:

- current state emphasis
- stage gates
- confidence/risk markers
- outcome labels for each phase
- visible transition from pilot to repeatable model

Avoid flat left-to-right bars where every phase has equal visual weight.

## Covers

Covers should respect the PowerPoint template geometry.

Good covers are quiet and sparse:

- deck type/date metadata
- large claim or title
- optional short body copy
- logo in approved position
- color bar when appropriate
- optional small semantic preview if it supports the deck concept

Avoid web hero panels, fake product screenshots, decorative right-side blobs, or unnecessary metadata systems.

## Dark Closes

Dark closes are useful for action and commitment.

Use:

- one strong ask
- 2-4 action steps or commitments
- sparse supporting line
- chartreuse or cyan numbering only when it helps scan

Avoid turning the close into a second agenda or a dense summary slide.

## Density Bands

Use density deliberately:

- `Executive sparse`: one claim, one visual, minimal support.
- `Executive standard`: claim plus diagram/proof strip and short note.
- `Working dense`: structured canvas, table, matrix, or swimlane with scannable labels.
- `Appendix dense`: detail-first, clearly marked as support/reference.

Do not let every slide sit in the middle. Strong decks alternate frame, argue, prove, synthesize, and reset.
