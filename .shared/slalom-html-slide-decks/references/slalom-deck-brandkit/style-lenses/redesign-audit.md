# Style Lens: Redesign Audit

Use this lens when refreshing, critiquing, or rebuilding an existing deck or generated output.

Best for:

- "make it less generic"
- "give it more design juice"
- "this looks too AI-generated"
- "audit this deck"
- "refresh the visual system"
- "why do these slides feel samey?"

## Audit Sequence

1. Identify the slide job and claim.
2. Identify the current visual move.
3. Check whether the move encodes meaning or only decorates text.
4. Scan for repeated primitives and layout sameness.
5. Check brand fidelity.
6. Recommend the smallest useful redesign move.

## Generic Slide Tells

Flag these directly:

- three equal cards used as the default answer
- repeated hub-spoke diagrams
- every slide split into left text and right boxes
- fake dashboards or fake app chrome
- generic AI imagery, glowing brains, robots, or mesh gradients
- crowded covers
- unprioritized tables
- icon cards replacing bullets without adding meaning
- excessive equal-weight boxes
- vague labels like "Overview", "Approach", "Key Benefits"
- decorative accents that do not map to meaning

## Redesign Moves

- Replace equal cards with a hierarchy: focal node, selected path, grouped clusters, or proof strip.
- Replace noun maps with flows, layers, dependencies, or ownership loops.
- Replace flat timelines with movement, stage gates, risk markers, or outcome bands.
- Replace dense paragraphs with claim headline plus visual support.
- Replace decorative color with semantic color.
- Add a reset slide when the deck needs rhythm.
- Split a dense slide when it has two jobs.

## Sameyness Audit

Across the deck, check:

- Are three or more adjacent slides using the same geometry?
- Are all comparisons two columns?
- Are all models central hub-spoke diagrams?
- Does every slide have the same title scale?
- Are all support elements equal-sized cards?
- Does each section have frame, argue, prove, synthesize, and reset moments?

## QA

Before finalizing, state:

- what was generic
- what changed
- why the new form better supports the claim
- what brand risks remain
- what slide still needs human review
