# Style Lens: Tactical Operational

Use this lens when the deck needs to feel operational, high-signal, and control-oriented without becoming fake military, terminal, or dark-tech decoration.

Best for:

- delivery control views
- risk and issue readouts
- governance dashboards
- PMO operating rhythm
- status, dependency, and decision visibility
- dense workshop artifacts

## Design Read

This lens reads as a Slalom delivery control surface: crisp grids, high scanability, explicit ownership, and dense but disciplined information.

## Visual Rules

- Use structured grids, tables, bands, and swimlanes.
- Use short metadata labels to help scanning.
- Use strong row/column hierarchy.
- Use Slalom Blue for primary state, ownership, or selected path.
- Use Coral Red sparingly for real risk or friction.
- Use Chartreuse or Cyan only for clear signal, not decoration.
- Prefer white canvas or Slalom dark blue fields; do not default to black terminal styling.

## Layout Moves

- ownership / leverage / signal matrix
- risk register summary with highlighted intervention
- workstream swimlane
- operating rhythm calendar band
- dependency map
- decision log excerpt
- control-tower overview with 3-5 zones

## Density Rules

- Dense is allowed when the slide is meant to operate from.
- Use labels, dividers, row grouping, and whitespace pockets to keep density readable.
- Keep text short and verb-led.
- Make exceptions, risks, and owners visually findable.

## Avoid

- fake terminals
- military/HUD decoration
- scanlines, crosshairs, classified-document aesthetics
- random status dots
- black-and-green command screens
- excessive monospace
- tiny text that only looks operational from far away

## QA

Before finalizing, ask:

- Can a reader find owner, risk, status, and next action quickly?
- Is the density useful or just impressive-looking?
- Are risk accents tied to real risk?
- Does the slide still feel like Slalom, not a generic control-room UI?
